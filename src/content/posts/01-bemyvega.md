## Projects

During my time at Bemyvega, I have learned many useful skills and had the opportunity to lead a professional team of developers during the design and development of numerous disruptive technologies in the sector. During this time, I have put into practice everything I know about team leadership, expanding my knowledge of how to coordinate a multidisciplinary team with experience.

After many years of learning the necessary theory on how to lead the joint efforts of an engineering team, I was presented with the opportunity to put my knowledge into practice following the sudden departure of the company's previous technical director.

Something very important to learn when taking on a position of responsibility is that performance at work is no longer due solely to one's own efforts. It is now the sum of all the efforts of the staff. Therefore, although I have earned merits, I must be completely honest and transparent. These successes are due to the passion and dedication of my colleagues, who have given their best for the company.

Among the milestones achieved during this time are:

- The creation of accessibility software for digital screens, which allows users at home to view the content displayed on a classroom screen, even from home or at the far end of the classroom, with first-rate accessibility tools.

- The implementation of artificial intelligence technologies to facilitate real-time transcription and translation in classrooms in the Murcia region. In addition, OCR technology was added to make what the speaker writes on the board more legible.

- Expansion of the functionalities of the product we developed to make websites more accessible (https://www.goviewser.com/). New features include a screen reader, an online dictionary, the ability to navigate with the keyboard (both with the keys and the tab key), the ability to adapt the page for people with disabilities, and the implementation of descriptive windows.

- Collaborating with the entire company on the migration of all our product websites.

- Restructuring and refactoring our conferencing and bidirectional platforms. Creating new platforms and developing new user management, organization, and session recording features. New ways of managing platform content, as well as restructuring and deploying new infrastructure.

- I prepared the entire company, both organizationally and technologically, to obtain ENS and ISO 27001 certifications.

- During my time as technical director, I made numerous decisions of vital importance to the company relating to the hiring and dismissal of staff.

Now that I have briefly discussed my contribution to the team and the achievements made during my year as technical director, I would like to talk about the company culture.

Throughout my life, I have never given much thought to something like “my company's culture.” To be honest, before getting involved in the subject, I always considered it nonsense. Pure corporate marketing to justify why they pay their employees minimum wage while serving pizza on Fridays. It was then, during a visit to the Madrid offices of our business partners, Amazon Web Services, that I had the opportunity to talk to someone who completely changed my opinion on the subject. That day, I met Crescencio Rodríguez, Director of Professional Services Iberia (AWS).

After a few conversations about marketing and product design for the following year, we went down to a restaurant near the office for lunch. The meal was relaxed, and we were able to talk about various topics, such as the education system in Spain. Finally, the moment came when all of us who were eating began to talk about corporate culture. As expected, they began to talk about their own values within AWS: **Think big, customer obsession, disagree and commit...**

At first, everything revolved around these vague ideas that still seemed like cheap marketing to me. It wasn't until Crescencio interrupted this whirlwind of ideas that someone finally caught my attention. With a simple sentence, he was able to perfectly define what corporate culture is, avoiding the flood of vague, marketing-like definitions that abound on LinkedIn. “**Corporate culture is what an employee has on Sunday night.**” That simple sentence changed me and basically defined what my corporate culture would be like from then on (and also what I want the culture to be like at any company I work for in the future). That's when I mentioned that when I worked at Capgemini, I always begged whatever deity would listen to me for Friday to come. And when it finally arrived, I prayed that Monday would never come. Sunday nights were traumatic during that stage of my life.

I discussed this situation with them, and we concluded that the culture of these companies, which hide behind social benefits such as meal vouchers and childcare discounts, ended up being more like slavery than a professional relationship.

A good corporate culture (which I would later promote together with my superiors at Bemyvega, and which I hope to promote wherever I go) has several fundamental values:

- First, work-life balance is essential. In fact, it is mandatory. Employees (and executives too) should have the right not only to disconnect from work once the working day is over (no text messages or emails at 2 a.m. ...), but also to have enough time outside working hours to develop a full life. A fulfilling life that allows them to develop as individuals, pursue their goals, enjoy their hobbies... a life they can actually live.

The opposite example to this point is precisely my experience at Capgemini, as well as that of close friends who, like me, have experienced working from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., thanks to “split shifts” and mandatory unpaid overtime. A good corporate culture encourages, whenever possible, an intensive, uninterrupted working day, which allows employees to avoid spending one to three hours in the office for which they are not paid. At Bemyvega, to encourage this, a working day from 8:00 a.m. to 3:45 p.m. was defined. This way, employees can freely enjoy their afternoons, which has led to a noticeable increase in their happiness and productivity (as shown by quarterly analyses).

- If a job can be done remotely, and it is even advisable from an economic point of view because it saves on the cost of physical space for employees, there are no compelling reasons not to facilitate remote working.

I understand what many executives say: “**I want to see my employees in person because that's how we build relationships, blah, blah, blah.**” In many cases (most cases), this obsession with the office is due to a lack of trust in employees or the inexperience of managers. Neither of these things is the fault of the employees, and they also make it difficult to achieve a work-life balance. If you want to build relationships between employees, and between employees and executives, you can always set aside a few days a month (two to four days, maybe once a week) for employees who want to meet at the office.

“No employee benefits can replace a decent wage, especially in times of inflation.” This was the phrase I used in my exit interview when I left Capgemini. If you want to retain talent in your company, you have to help employees lead a decent life. Life, due to inflation, is becoming increasingly expensive, and the situation does not seem to be improving. Even access to housing is becoming increasingly unrealistic for employees of many companies. A good corporate culture promotes fair rewards for employees. You can even consider rewarding high-performing employees who show a special interest and passion for their work. If employees do not earn enough to live a decent life (I don't mean being able to buy a car every month. I mean not drowning in bills). I mean being able to afford things like buying quality food, or being able to pay the rent or mortgage every month and have enough to save, they will want to leave. And those who can't won't work with all the performance and enthusiasm that can be achieved.

- Fire the brilliant geniuses. In many companies, especially smaller ones (and, on rare occasions, very large ones), there is always a toxic employee, sometimes lazy, who undermines the morale of their team and always carries out actions that harm the company (constantly criticizing the efforts of others, not working properly, increasing the workload of their colleagues, but still collecting their salary).

Sometimes these employees are not fired because of their close relationship with an executive or because decision-makers do not dare to fire anyone. Sometimes it is even because, despite being toxic people, they are considered “geniuses” who are “indispensable” to the company. A good corporate culture rewards a situation that is fair to everyone. No employee who harms the company or their colleagues with their behavior should remain on the payroll, as otherwise their colleagues may perceive that, without making an effort, they will also receive their salary every month and therefore it is not worth working. As the saying goes, “without punishment, we spoil the bandits.”

- Show employees that their work has an impact on the company. A good corporate culture shows employees that their work is important, which helps keep them motivated. It's difficult to give your all at work if you feel like you're just a cog in a machine much bigger than yourself. A good corporate culture helps employees see firsthand the impact their work has. This is complicated, as it depends on the nature of each employee's work and the company. It is much more difficult for a large company to show an accountant the enormous impact their working hours have.

In summary, these are the key points for creating a good corporate culture. After applying them, I have managed to get my entire team to constantly tell me how happy they are to work at bemyvega, and we have managed to increase the number of features developed and the overall happiness of the company, not just the engineering team.

In my humble opinion, during my time as CTO of bemyvega (while maintaining my role as a DevOps Cloud engineer), I have achieved great success.

Conditions have improved greatly. It's true that, during the first few months, my job consisted of reversing the disaster caused by the previous CTO and reestablishing relationships with our business partners (the previous CTO was about to be sued for abuse, and several business partners indicated that they did not want to meet with him again due to his repeated disrespect). Once I finished resolving these issues, project development accelerated greatly.

(It is worth adding that the previous technical director even threatened the entire engineering team that if they couldn't get the job done easily, he would find someone else to do it. Faced with his constant threats, the team found it difficult to motivate themselves to keep going).

## Personal opinion

Bemyvega has become my second home and the best company I have ever worked for, in my two current positions. The CEO has always treated me with great respect and supported my decisions. He has always trusted my judgment, and my team and I have been rewarded for all the dedication we have put into Bemyvega's projects.

> Rating: **10/10**. I highly recommend working for this company. The conditions are impressive and the management team is very open and communicative. The work environment is positive, healthy, and relaxed.
